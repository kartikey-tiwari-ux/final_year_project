# Research Gap

Grounded in the sources verified in `LITERATURE_REVIEW.md`.

## 1. What has already been done?
- **Text-only mental-health-related classification** is well established: domain-adapted transformers (e.g., MentalBERT, Ji et al. 2022) reliably outperform general-purpose BERT/RoBERTa and biomedical/clinical variants on Reddit-sourced mental-health benchmarks.
- **Image-only emotion recognition** is well established for facial-expression data using transfer learning from pretrained CNNs (Agung et al. 2024: 96% accuracy, 0.95 F1 with fine-tuned Inception-V3 on the Emognition dataset).
- **Multimodal sentiment analysis** has a mature methodological taxonomy — two independent systematic reviews (Gandhi et al. 2023; Das & Singh 2023) catalogue early/late/attention-based fusion strategies, and publicly available, real, substantially-sized text+image social-media benchmarks exist (MVSA, Niu et al. 2016: 5,129–19,600 Twitter image-text pairs).
- At least one directly comparable prior effort (Vasanthi & Viswanatham, 2026) has built a hybrid image+text multimodal sentiment classifier and reported strong aggregate metrics (F-measure 0.927, MCC 0.904).

## 2. What are the limitations of this existing work?
- **Representativeness and sampling bias:** a 2024 systematic review of 47 ML-for-mental-illness-detection studies (Cao et al.) found Twitter dominates as a data source (63.8%), >90% of studies are English-only, 80% use non-probability sampling, and roughly a quarter do not use imbalance-aware evaluation metrics — meaning headline accuracy numbers in this literature are frequently not safely comparable across studies.
- **Scale mismatch in multimodal social-media evaluation:** the most directly relevant prior multimodal sentiment paper (Vasanthi & Viswanatham, 2026) evaluates its social-media (Twitter) condition on only 100 image-text pairs, far smaller than its other two datasets (1,042 and 1,514 images) — the multimodal vs. unimodal comparison on *real social-media* data specifically is thin, not the overall study.
- **Benchmark domain mismatch:** the most architecturally relevant attention-fusion work found (Jiang & Ji, 2022, CMGA) is validated on CMU-MOSI/MOSEI — video+audio+text utterance-level benchmarks — not static social-media image+text posts, so fusion designs tuned there may not transfer directly.
- **Image-emotion literature skew:** most verified image-only emotion work is facial-expression-centric (Emognition/FER-style datasets), not general social-media imagery (memes, scenes, objects, text-in-image), which is what posts in a dataset like MVSA actually contain.
- **Architectural data-hungriness vs. available compute:** ViT-style architectures (Dosovitskiy et al. 2021) need large-scale pretraining data to beat CNNs and underperform CNNs when trained from scratch on modest datasets — a documented limitation directly relevant to resource-constrained reproductions of this research.

## 3. What remains insufficiently studied?
- A **controlled, same-dataset, same-metrics comparison** of text-only vs. image-only vs. multimodal classification specifically on a substantially-sized (thousands of samples), publicly available, real social-media sentiment dataset — most multimodal work either evaluates on a tiny social-media subset (Vasanthi & Viswanatham's 100 pairs) or on non-social-media benchmarks (MOSI/MOSEI, product-review images).
- **Whether multimodal fusion actually helps, and under what conditions it does not**, reported honestly — much of the literature reports multimodal models winning, but the underlying evaluation conditions (dataset size, domain match) vary enough that a fair "does fusion help on *this* kind of data" answer is not reliably established for Twitter-style image+text sentiment at the sample sizes feasible for a single student project.
- **Resource-constrained reproducibility**: most cited work assumes GPU-scale compute and does not document what happens (architecture choices, accuracy trade-offs) when the same comparison is attempted on CPU-only hardware with limited disk — relevant to any student/practitioner without institutional compute access.
- **Explainability specifically for the fusion decision** (not just unimodal explainability) — Grad-CAM (image) and transformer attention/SHAP/LIME (text) are each well studied in isolation; how much each modality contributes to a *multimodal* model's specific prediction is less consistently reported in the papers reviewed here.

## 4. What will our project investigate?
1. A same-dataset, same-split, same-metrics comparison of **Model A (text-only)**, **Model B (image-only)**, and **Model C (multimodal fusion)** on a real, substantially-sized, publicly documented text+image social-media sentiment dataset (MVSA family — final choice and justification in `DATASET_SELECTION.md`), reporting accuracy, precision, recall, macro/weighted F1, and confusion matrices for all three.
2. Whether multimodal fusion improves over the better unimodal baseline on this specific data — reported as found, including the possibility that it does not, per the project's own non-negotiable rule against assuming or manufacturing a multimodal win.
3. A resource-constrained reproduction: documenting which architecture choices (e.g., frozen pretrained CNN vs. ViT; compact transformer encoder vs. full BERT) are necessary under CPU-only/limited-disk conditions, and what that costs in performance versus the GPU-scale literature.
4. Basic explainability for each branch (text and image) and, where feasible, a qualitative look at modality contribution/conflict in the fused model, framed as exploratory rather than a rigorously validated attribution method.

## 5. Why is the proposed experiment meaningful?
It does not claim to be the first multimodal sentiment system — the literature clearly shows this is an active, well-populated area. Its value is in **closing the specific, verifiable gap** identified above: a rigorous, same-conditions unimodal-vs-multimodal comparison on real, adequately-sized social-media image+text data (not a 100-sample subset, not a non-social-media benchmark), built and evaluated honestly under realistic resource constraints, with results reported as they come out — including a null or negative result for multimodal fusion if that is what the experiment shows. This gives a small but genuine, defensible, and reproducible data point to a field where many comparable claims are made on thin or domain-mismatched evaluation conditions.
